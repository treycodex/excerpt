# Excerpt — agency product intent

Updated September 13, 2026 from the user's requested audience shift.
Status: current product direction; positioning implementation remains TODO.

## Who it is for

Agency-side marketers who discuss work on shared screens: creative strategists,
designers, creative leads, media planners/buyers, performance marketers, and account
or strategy leads responsible for carrying feedback into the next round of work.

The initial user is an individual attending a creative, media, or client review
who needs a reliable record to act on afterwards. The initial product remains an
individual meeting companion; this direction does not imply team workspaces,
client portals, or agency administration features.

## Core job

Leave a campaign review knowing what was shown, what people said about it, and
what was agreed next—without reconstructing the call from disconnected notes,
screenshots, decks, and reports.

Positioning: **Meeting memory for creative and media agencies.**
Promise: **Keep the creative, the numbers, and the conversation together.**

Reports are first-class visual meeting context alongside creative assets. The
job includes understanding why a team chose its next action, not just preserving
what a design looked like.

## Priority workflows

| Workflow | Screen context | What the user needs afterwards |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Creative review | Ad variants, video frames, copy, storyboard, landing page | Feedback attached to the correct asset/version, requested revisions, and explicit approvals or unresolved questions |
| Media/performance review | Dashboard, campaign table, channel report, budget sheet | The numbers shown, the team's interpretation, and the actual optimization or budget decision |
| Client reporting call | Results deck, charts, recommendations | Client questions, caveats, approvals, and agreed follow-up with the relevant slide |
| Internal creative/media handoff | Brief, media plan, test results, creative examples | Why the next iteration is needed, what to change/test, and any explicitly agreed owner or deadline |

These are connected uses of one meeting-memory workflow, not four product modes.
Lead with a campaign review that connects a performance report to a creative
revision; validate other workflows after that initial use case.

## Example of the value

A media buyer shows a campaign report and explains that one creative has weaker
click-through performance during the displayed reporting period. The creative
lead opens that asset, and the team agrees to test a clearer opening message.

The useful record contains the captured report with its period and filters
visible, the specific creative being discussed, the relevant conversation, and an
editable note of the agreed test. The user can revisit the passage to distinguish
an observation from a hypothesis, recommendation, or approved change.

This is an illustrative workflow, not evidence of measured customer demand or a
claim that the app can interpret report pixels automatically.

## Product implications and boundaries

- The unit users want to remember is an asset/report moment plus its discussion
  and outcome. Screenshots should retain enough labels to identify the client,
  campaign, asset/version, date range, channel, filters, metric, and units when those
  are available on screen. Users can supply missing context in editable captions.
- Treat a spoken explanation and a visible report as distinct sources. A nearby
  quote does not verify a number shown in an image. Preserve uncertainty when the
  two disagree; do not silently reconcile them or invent a causal explanation.
- An idea, proposed spend change, or client comment is not automatically an
  approval or assigned task. Existing conservative extraction remains appropriate.
- The current screenshot/paste workflow can retain report views as images. It does
  not establish PDF/spreadsheet import, OCR, report parsing, metric verification,
  dashboard synchronization, or automatic analysis. Those require separate scope
  decisions and implementation before any marketing promise.
- Use synthetic agency assets and report data for public examples. Keep existing
  local-default and explicit optional-cloud behavior accurately described.
- Do not automatically add ad-platform integrations, report generation, attribution
  analysis, project management, or cross-client search to this audience shift.

## Validation questions

When target participants are available, observe creative, media, and account-side
users reviewing a realistic campaign example. Ask them to retrieve the correct
asset, reporting period, client feedback, and agreed next action from the notes.
Record where they still reconstruct context or mistake suggestions for approvals.
Test whether the captured report remains readable and useful after export.

These are research questions for the direction, not a claim that validation has
already occurred or an expansion of the authorized top-three implementation.
